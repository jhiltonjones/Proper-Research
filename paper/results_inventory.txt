  - Several reps in the original n=5 -> n=10 extension campaign (MPC-SJ/FJ,
    InvJac-SJ/FJ) needed retries, mostly from the known reproducible rectangle
    MPC-frozen workspace-violation failure mode (documented separately —
    frozen Jacobian is structurally less reliable on the rectangle's sharper
    corners) and one anomalous joint-configuration excursion on rectangle
    MPC-SJ rep8 that required manual robot repositioning before resuming.

Key findings already reported to the user:
  - Closed-loop beats open-loop on both shapes, all controllers/conditions.
  - MPC(N=15) vs InvJac: mixed — rectangle favors MPC-scheduled clearly
    (0.464 vs 0.598mm), triangle slightly favors MPC-frozen/InvJac over
    MPC-scheduled (frozen-Jacobian MPC is actually tighter than scheduled on
    the triangle specifically).
  - Frozen vs scheduled MPC: scheduled clearly wins on rectangle; frozen
    slightly wins on triangle (both small effects except rectangle's large gap).
  - MPC horizon N=1 vs N=15: N=15 wins decisively on both shapes and with
    both schedule types. N=1's cost structure (R=700x weight pulling the
    command back toward the feedforward reference input, no nullspace/
    anchor regularization to compensate) has no multi-step budget to spread
    a correction over, so N=1 collapses close to open-loop behavior
    (rectangle MPC-N1 RMSE 1.87-1.94mm vs open-loop's 1.90mm) rather than
    behaving like a weaker reactive controller. InvJac has no such
    "stay-near-feedforward" cost term and reacts with full proportional
    authority every tick regardless of horizon, which is why it beats
    MPC-N1 by a wide margin on both shapes.

Comparability caveats (important for how these are presented in the paper):
  - 1 vs 1b is NOT a clean open-loop comparison -- different solved paths.
  - 3 vs 4 (MPC N=15 vs N=1, both on the base/nodipole plan) IS a clean
    same-plan horizon comparison: N=15 beats N=1 by a wide margin (0.701mm
    vs 1.871mm RMSE median), consistent with the same mechanism identified
    in the shape work -- N=1 has no multi-step budget to spread a
    correction over and collapses toward open-loop-like behavior (though
    less severely here than on the rectangle shape, since vessel open-loop
    itself is much worse at 3.656mm -- the vessel's contact dynamics make
    open-loop fail harder than any closed-loop variant regardless of
    horizon).
  - 5 vs 3 crosses plans (dipole-aligned vs base) -- do not present as a
    clean "dipole-alignment helps/hurts" result without that caveat; at
    face value they are nearly identical (0.708mm vs 0.701mm RMSE median),
    which itself is worth noting but should be framed as "comparable
    performance across the two solved paths," not "dipole-alignment has no
    effect."
